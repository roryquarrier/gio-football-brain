# 📊 Step 4: Expected Goals (xG)
**⏱️ 9:00 → 12:00 (3 minutes)**

## 🎯 Goal

Use **one number** to judge shots better than the score or possession.

---

## 🧠 What is xG?

> ## xG = the **probability** that a shot becomes a goal — a number from **0 to 1**.

It is calculated from thousands of similar shots in the past, based on things like
**distance**, **angle**, **body part** (foot vs head) and the type of chance.

| Shot | Approx. xG | In words |
|---|---|---|
| Penalty | **~0.76** | about 3 out of 4 go in |
| Tap-in near the goal line | **0.6 – 0.9** | almost a goal |
| Shot from the edge of the box | **~0.05 – 0.10** | 1 in 10–20 |
| Long shot from 30 metres | **~0.02** | 1 in 50 |

## ➕ How to use it: add them up

Team A takes 3 shots with xG **0.40, 0.10, 0.05** → total **0.55 xG**.
That means: on average, those shots *should* produce about **half a goal**.

## 🧠 Why this beats the final score

Football has **few goals**, so luck matters. A team can lose 0-1 while creating
**2.4 xG against 0.4 xG**.

- In **one game**, that's usually just bad luck.
- Over **a season** (38 matches), xG is a **better predictor of future results** than goals scored.

## ⚠️ xG has limits

- It says how **good the chance** was, not how good the **finisher** is. A world-class striker may score *more* than his xG, year after year.
- It doesn't see **the goalkeeper's position** unless it's a more advanced model.
- **Possession ≠ winning.** A team can have **70% of the ball** and **0.3 xG** — passing around the box without a real chance.

> ## 🎵 Say this:
> ## Shots aren't equal. **Chance quality** beats chance **quantity**.
